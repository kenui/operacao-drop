First Hunt Guide · for cats

Chase the light.
Then catch something real.

How to set up the first night, what to do if your cat ignores it, and how to end every session with a real catch.

Before you switch it on (2 minutes)

  • Dim the room. Evening with a lamp, or curtains closed. In daylight the shapes are faint.
  • Clear some floor. About six feet of bare, smooth floor in front of the projector.
  • Set it low and steady. On the floor or a low table, pointed at the open floor.
  • Put the catnip mouse in your pocket. You'll need it at the end.

The first night

  • Switch it on, then sit down and stay quiet. Don't carry your cat to it or push her face toward the light.
  • Most cats watch before they move: a long stare, a low crouch, the tail tip twitching, the back end wiggling. That's interest. Give her three to five minutes before you decide anything.
  • If she pounces, let her. If she looks under her paw for the mouse that isn't there, that's normal. It's exactly why the plush is in the box.

The 10-minute hunt

0:00Lights low, projector on, you on the couch or the floor.
0:00 to 8:00She chases. You don't need to do anything.
When she slows downSitting, lying down, grooming, or looking at you: that's the signal. Switch it off.
Right awayToss the catnip mouse a foot or two in front of her. Let her grab it, kick it, carry it off.
AfterA meal or a treat. Cats tend to follow a hunt, catch, eat, groom, sleep pattern, and a snack closes the loop.
Why end with the plush?
Light is great for the chase, but she can never pin it down. Ending with something she can actually catch gives the game a finish. Many vets and feline behavior resources suggest exactly that for any light toy.

If she ignores it

  • Make it darker. A lamp in the corner is fine; daylight washes the shapes out.
  • Use bare, smooth floor. Busy rugs hide the mice.
  • Angle it so the shapes run away from her, not at her. Prey runs away.
  • Try before dinner, when a lot of cats are more in the mood to hunt.
  • Sit on the floor with her. Some cats play more when you're at their level.
  • Try a few different nights. Some cats warm up slowly.

If she gets too wound up

  • End sooner and always finish with the plush mouse, so the game has an ending.
  • If she starts chasing reflections or shadows afterward, shorten the sessions and keep them to a set time of day.
  • Don't leave it running while you're out of the room.

Keep it new

Novelty matters. Three or four sessions a week usually keep it interesting longer than every day. Put the catnip mouse away between sessions so it stays special. Rub it between your hands before tossing it to wake up the catnip smell.

A note for your evenings

If your cat runs the halls at 3 a.m., try a session about an hour before your bedtime, followed by her meal. It may help her settle at night. Every cat is different, so give it a week before you judge.

Safety

  • Use it with you in the room. Don't leave it on unattended.
  • Don't let anyone look straight into the lens.
  • Keep any cable out of reach of a cat who chews.
  • Check the plush for loose seams or parts now and then, and replace it when it's worn.
  • Catnip: many adult cats react to it, but some never do, and kittens under about six months usually don't. If she ignores the smell, that's genetics, not the toy.
