First Hunt Guide · for dogs

Chase the light.
Then catch something real.

How to set up the first night, what to do if your dog ignores it or barks at it, and how to end every session with a real toy in his mouth.

Before you switch it on (2 minutes)

  • Dim the room. Evening with a lamp, or curtains closed. In daylight the shapes are faint.
  • Clear some floor. About six feet of bare, smooth floor in front of the projector.
  • Set it low and steady. On the floor or a low table, pointed at the open floor.
  • Put the squeaky squirrel in your pocket. You'll need it at the end.

The first night

  • Switch it on, then sit down. Let him find it on his own.
  • Dogs often sniff first: the projector, then the circles of light. A play bow (front down, back end up), a bounce to the side, or one sharp bark all mean he's figuring it out.
  • Some dogs go straight to chasing, others need a couple of evenings. Both are normal.

The 10-minute hunt

0:00Lights low, projector on, you on the couch.
0:00 to 8:00He chases from circle to circle.
When he slows downOr before he gets too wound up: switch it off.
Right awayDrop the squeaky squirrel in front of him. Let him grab it, shake it, squeak it.
AfterIf you want the toy back, trade it for a treat and say "drop it". Then calm time: a chew, his bed, water.
Why end with the plush?
Lights alone never give him the part he likes best: something to grab and shake. Ending with the squeaky toy gives the game a clear finish, so the light going off doesn't feel like the fun just vanished.

If he ignores it

  • Make it darker. A lamp is fine; daylight washes the critters out.
  • Use bare, smooth floor. Busy rugs hide the shapes.
  • Point to the shapes and get excited with him for the first minute. Many dogs follow your energy.
  • Try after a short sniff walk, not when he's exhausted or just ate.
  • Try a few different evenings.
  • Some dogs just aren't interested in lights. That's what the 30-day refund is for. Email us.

If he gets too wound up

  • Some dogs bark at the lights at first. Keep sessions short (five minutes is fine) and end while he's still having fun, not when he's frantic.
  • Always finish with the squeaky toy so the game has an ending.
  • If he starts chasing reflections or shadows around the house afterward, cut back and keep sessions to a set time of day.
  • We can't promise a quiet dog. If barking doesn't settle down after a few sessions, this may not be the toy for him.

Keep it new

Novelty matters. Three or four sessions a week usually keep it exciting longer than every day. Put the squeaky squirrel away between sessions so it stays the special toy.

A note for your evenings

Rainy day, no walk? A short session in the late afternoon, then a chew in his bed, gives him something to do in the living room. It won't replace a walk, but it beats staring at you for an hour.

Safety

  • Use it with you in the room. Don't leave it on unattended.
  • Big, excited dogs can knock it over or try to chew it. Put it on a low, stable spot out of tail range.
  • Don't let anyone look straight into the lens.
  • Keep any cable out of reach of a dog who chews.
  • Squeaky toys aren't chew toys. Take it away when the squeaker comes out or the seams open, and replace it.
